## Follow-up discovery and HyperSkill review

A second Aug 18 pass widened discovery over newly indexed Aug 17 work and accepted **HyperSkill: Self-Evolving LLM Agents via Hypergraph-Structured Skill Memory (2608.16114)**.

| Paper | Primary category | Relevance | Importance | Decision |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| HyperSkill (2608.16114) | Memory Learning & Evolution | 0.99 | 4/5 | Accept; higher-order trajectory structure is actually consumed by dual-path retrieval and maintenance. Strong ablations support the whole structural pipeline, but representation and access are not fully isolated. |

Full-paper review checked the hypergraph schema, subtask-level and trajectory-level retrieval paths, co-occurrence skill ranking, utility update, periodic pruning/merging, two-backbone main results, token analysis, sensitivity study, and component ablations. On Qwen3-30B-A3B the full model reports **52.00 / 36.97 / 50.59** success on xBench / GAIA / WebWalkerQA, versus **41.00 / 35.76 / 44.71** without the hypergraph structural pipeline, **43.00 / 32.73 / 47.06** without subtask retrieval, and **48.00 / 35.76 / 43.53** without trajectory retrieval.

The skeptical correction is central to the 4/5 rather than 5/5 rating: `w/o hypergraph` replaces the entire dual-path structural pipeline with flat skill retrieval, so it does not isolate the causal value of n-ary hypergraph representation from the access procedure built around it. A flat or binary-graph store with the same decomposition, two retrieval paths, co-occurrence ranking, and maintenance budget remains the missing control.

No code/project URL was published because no official repository was independently verified.
