# SkillJack: Persistent Skill Backdoors in Self-Evolving Agents

**arXiv:** [2608.03509](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03509) · **Published:** 2026-08-04 · **Category:** Evaluation & Analysis · **Importance:** 4/5 · **AI confidence:** High

**Tags:** `procedural` `structured` `general-agent` · **Code:** [Tencent/AI-Infra-Guard — SkillJack](https://github.com/Tencent/AI-Infra-Guard/tree/main/Research/SkillJack)

> **TL;DR.** SkillJack shows that poisoned experience can be transformed by an agent's own experience-to-skill pipeline into a separately stored behavioral artifact that becomes harder to detect and can survive deletion of its source record.

## Problem

Memory-poisoning work usually assumes the poisoned record matters when it is retrieved later. Self-evolving agents create a different lifecycle: experience can be **compiled into a reusable skill**. If trust is checked only on the source record, the derived artifact can outlive that decision.

## Core idea

Inject functionally framed poisoned trajectories and let unmodified experience-to-skill systems compile them. SkillJack measures three properties that exist specifically across this transformation boundary: **sanitization whitewashing** (the skill looks safer than its source), **cross-layer promotion** (transient experience becomes a routable capability), and **persistence isolation** (deleting the source does not delete the skill).

## Memory design

| Stage | Design |
|---|---|
| Write | Attacker influences only the experience corpus; the system itself extracts structured skills from those records. |
| Organize | Derived skills live in a separate persistent library, decoupled from their source trajectories. |
| Read | Native/system-specific routing selects skills for later tasks; the evaluation tracks targeted and collateral triggering. |
| Update / forget | Source deletion does not imply descendant revocation; the paper argues for provenance propagation and descendant-aware cleanup. |

## Compared to what

Unlike direct memory poisoning, the source record does **not** need to be retrieved at attack time. The important comparison is a lifecycle one: `poisoned record → later retrieval` versus `poisoned record → transformation → persistent skill → later routing`. The paper also compares against direct-context injection and preliminary extraction-time/runtime defenses.

## Evidence

On SkillX, the LLM judge flags **98.5%** of framed poisoned trajectories but only **11.4%** of their extracted skills. A second system, Anything2Skill, shows the same qualitative whitewashing with different extraction rates. Routing-level policy-violation proxies trigger on **56.2%** of attack-oriented SkillX tasks and **89.2%** in the A2S DOC evaluation. In the matched deletion experiment, **80.0%** of skill-mediated triggers persist after source records are removed.

The caveat matters: SkillX also shows a **20.0% clean-task trigger rate**, so the attack is not tightly targeted. Most ASR numbers measure routing-level proxy violations rather than live compromise.

## Why it matters

SkillJack expands memory security from **record integrity** to **artifact lineage integrity**. If memories are summarized, compiled, distilled, or promoted into skills, provenance and revocation need to follow the descendants across every transformation boundary.

## Limitations / questions

- Two experience-to-skill systems are evaluated under one DeepSeek-v4-flash configuration; cross-model generalization is unknown.
- Most downstream success is a routing-level policy-violation proxy, with only a very small code-generation verification and no live external execution.
- The 150-trajectory dataset is controlled and synthesized from AppWorld-style patterns rather than independently collected experience.
- Defense experiments are small and partly post-hoc; they support design hypotheses rather than established defenses.

**Curator take:** **4/5**. The strongest contribution is the lifecycle failure mode, not the headline ASR: derived artifacts can become less inspectable and outlive their provenance unless revocation is transitive.
