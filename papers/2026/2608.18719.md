# Competence, Not Accuracy: A Diagnostic for Reference-Free Judge Gates in Skill Optimization

[← Latest Papers](../../README.md#latest-papers) · [Research Map](../../README.md#research-map) · [Evaluation & Analysis](../../categories/evaluation-analysis.md) · [中文](2608.18719.zh.md)

**Paper:** [arXiv:2608.18719](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18719) · **Published:** 2026-08-19 · **Importance:** 4/5 · **Confidence:** High
**Tags:** `procedural` `text` `structured` `general-agent`

> **Research delta.** Before an LLM judge is allowed to admit persistent skill edits, test whether it can discriminate correct from incorrect attempts on the same questions. Marginal benchmark accuracy can look strong while the within-question gate signal is near chance.

## Problem

Text-space skill optimization proposes a new natural-language skill document and uses a gate to decide whether it replaces the persistent version. Automatic verifiers limit the task domains; a reference-free judge broadens coverage but can also commit harmful state.

## Mechanism

The work models a judge as a latent solver, derives the competence floor `c > 1/k`, and attaches a non-intervening probe to genuine SkillOpt runs. The probe records judge scores without changing decisions, then uses verifier labels to estimate **within-question ROC-AUC**, removing item-difficulty confounding.

## Compared with

The diagnostic compares marginal and within-question AUC on the same optimization traces. Closed-loop experiments then compare verifier, judge, and random gates. This is a gate-quality study—not evidence that judge-driven skill optimization is generally better.

## Decisive evidence

Research math yields **0.457 marginal / 0.489 within-question AUC**. Factual QA falls from **0.855** marginal to **0.735** within-question, a 0.12 difficulty confound. A GPQA headline of **0.840** falls to **0.626** with empty answers and **0.547** under a matched proxy.

The cross-provider study repeats the near-floor competence / near-random gate pattern. In closed loop, the diagnostic predicts harmful-acceptance versus harmful-rejection error type more reliably than final aggregate performance.

## Main caveat

Passing the competence floor is necessary, not sufficient. The analysis needs repeated correct/incorrect attempts for the same questions and retrospective correctness labels. Tasks have one gold answer, so open-ended judging remains unresolved. An early bare-answer-letter probe also produced a false null, showing that the measurement protocol itself can fail.

## Memory lifecycle

| Stage | Design |
|---|---|
| Write | SkillOpt proposes a revised natural-language skill document. |
| Organize | Candidate and accepted versions form persistent procedural state. |
| Read | A shadow judge probe scores genuine attempts; verifier labels audit discriminability. |
| Update / forget | The gate admits or rejects the candidate; rollback, lineage, and retirement are outside scope. |

## Why it matters

A promotion gate should be qualified on the distribution of candidates it will actually govern. Judge accuracy on other questions cannot substitute for same-question separation at the state-commit boundary.
