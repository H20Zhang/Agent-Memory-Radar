# Optimal Skill Selection for LLM Agents with Provable Bicriteria Guarantees

**Paper:** [arXiv:2608.19993](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19993) · **Published:** 2026-08-20 · **Importance:** 4/5 · **Confidence:** Medium
**Tags:** `procedural` `text` `structured` `general-agent`

> **Research delta.** BPS treats skill access as a budgeted set problem: complementarity, redundancy, and context penalty are properties of the selected slate, not independent relevance scores. The practical win depends on extensive outcome supervision and a capability-gated fixed benchmark.

## Problem

Top-k retrieval scores each skill independently, although two documents can be useful only together, redundant together, or harmful once their context cost is counted.

## Mechanism

Historical executions fit nonnegative query-demand and skill-supply vectors. A monotone submodular benefit minus token penalty scores whole skill sets, and Best Prefix Selection chooses a slate under a hard budget with a bicriteria guarantee on the surrogate objective. The fixed skill documents then enter a frozen executor's prompt.

## Compared with

Direct set interventions compare no skill, one skill, complementary pairs, redundancy, and irrelevant procedure. Value-model controls share the same 63,596 execution records; search controls share the same fitted objective. Released router comparisons share the executor and library, but not BPS's supervision or optimization budget.

## Decisive evidence

In a controlled capability case, either one skill gives **0%**, the complementary pair **93%**, adding a redundant 225-token skill **94%**, and adding an irrelevant 385-token skill drops success to **56%**. Selected documents therefore causally change later execution.

End-to-end lookup BPS reaches **.73** task success versus **.20–.52** for released routers, retrievers, and self-selection, using **28% fewer tokens** than the strongest released router. The same-record and same-objective ablations isolate the model/search components more cleanly than the headline comparison.

## Main caveat

The private BigCodeBench variant admits tasks only when the standard solution and every one-capability hybrid fail, making no-skill success zero by construction and favoring complementarity. BPS learns from **63,596 Qwen3-32B executions**; baseline routers are not retrained on the same data. The theorem covers the fitted surrogate, not true success.

The neural deployment variant still needs annotated warm-up and online outcomes. Selection latency, training tokens, GPU-hours, dollars, storage, cold-start, library maintenance, and energy are unreported; saved prompt tokens may not repay ingestion cost.

## Why it matters

Procedural-memory access should be evaluated as set selection under cost, while charging the supervision needed to learn that set value.
